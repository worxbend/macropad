Twelve keys, two knobs and a line of light.

An open-source macropad on its own four-layer board, with a nice!view screen between the knobs and a thin glow in the seam of the case. Every file to make one is in the repository.

Download the order files Open the repository

Not built yet. Nothing here has been printed, milled, assembled or switched on. The pictures are renders of the design files.

One slab, one seam

The case is two shells that meet in a shadow gap halfway up its sides. Light from eight LEDs on the board edge leaks into that gap, so the pad glows along a single line instead of through strips. Make it on a printer or have it milled from aluminium; both come from the same parametric model.

Signature edition

The underside of the milled case carries a hand-drawn signature and the words Signature Edition, laser-marked into the anodised surface beside the centre foot.

The mark is flat artwork, not a cut: the laser shop gets it as a drawing at its place on the base.

A real board, not hand wiring

An RP2354A, the RP2350 with its flash inside the package, sits on a four-layer board with every key on its own pin. Hot-swap sockets take any MX switch. One USB-C port powers it, talks to the computer and loads new firmware.

The whole board is generated by code, routed by its own router and checked against its schematic on every build. Read the schematic.

Firmware you can read

Written in Rust on embassy. Four layers for media, editing, navigation and numbers. The screen shows what each key does right now, and the knobs change volume, scroll or anything you map. Drag one file onto the board to update it.

What is in it

Keys
12, MX hot-swap, one pin each, no diodes
Knobs
2 Alps EC11 encoders with push
Screen
nice!view, 160 × 68, always on
Light
8 side-emitting RGB LEDs in the case seam
Chip
RP2354A: RP2350 with 2 MB flash in the package
Board
84 × 82.5 mm, four layers, parts on one side
Port
One USB-C for power, data and flashing
Firmware
Rust, embassy, drag-and-drop UF2 updates

    Upload the Gerbers, bill of materials and placement list to JLCPCB. They place every part but three.

    Ordering guide

  2. Make the case

    Print the STL files, or send the STEP files to a CNC shop together with the signature drawing.

    Case notes

  3. Put it together

    Solder the knobs and the display socket, fit the switches, close the case with six screws from below.

    Assembly guide

  4. Load the firmware

    Hold BOOT, plug it in and copy macropad.uf2 to the drive that appears.
